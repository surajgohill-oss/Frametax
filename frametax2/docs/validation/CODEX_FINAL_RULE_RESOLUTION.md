# Codex Final Rule Resolution

**As of:** 9 August 2026  
**Scope:** Only the high-value questions carried forward in `UNRESOLVED_JURISDICTION_RULES.md`. This is an authority resolution, not a new jurisdiction audit. No other provider's conclusions or response files were used.

## Executive resolution

All 14 determinations below are **RESOLVED**. The principal canonical corrections are: Mauritius has no separate 35% tier and its 90%-production condition belongs to a different tax measure; Greece does impose the 80%-of-total-production-cost ceiling; UK IFTC is enhanced AVEC rather than a separate statutory credit; Malta's uplift has two 5% limbs but remains Commissioner-awarded; and none of the five territories treats payment through a local SPV as sufficient by itself.

## 1. Mauritius

### 1.1 Is the 40% tier additionally conditioned on 90% local filming?

- **Rule/question:** Whether 40% Film Rebate Scheme treatment requires at least 90% of the film to be produced in Mauritius.
- **Authoritative answer:** **No.** The 90% condition appears in the Government's 2023/24 Budget Measures for a separate **double deduction** available to local companies financing, sponsoring, marketing or distributing an approved film. The current EDB Film Rebate guidance states 30%, or up to 40% for qualifying high-end productions, on QPE incurred locally; it does not attach a 90% production test to the rebate uplift.
- **Exact source:** [EDB Budget Measures 2023/24, p. 4](https://www.edbmauritius.org/budget2023/Annex_Budget_2023-2024.pdf); [EDB Film Rebate Scheme submission guidance, p. 3](https://edbmauritius.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Guideline-Online-Application-FRS.pdf); [current EDB Creative Industry page](https://edbmauritius.org/creative-industry).
- **Effective date/version:** Budget measure 2023/24; EDB submission guidance version dated 31 January 2020; EDB program page current when checked 9 August 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Do not impose a 90%-local-production gate on the 40% Film Rebate rate. Keep the separate double-deduction measure out of the rebate calculation unless it is modeled independently.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical rule; it is not a Little Utopia factual election.

### 1.2 What is the authoritative relationship between 30%, 35% and 40%?

- **Rule/question:** Whether these are three official tiers.
- **Authoritative answer:** They are not. **30% is the ordinary rate.** For a feature film or drama episode satisfying the published minimum QPE, the award may be **more than 30% and no more than 40%** where the production brings significant economic benefit, at the Film Rebate Committee's recommended percentage and subject to requirements it determines. This is a discretionary continuum, not published 30/35/40 bands.
- **Exact source:** [Mauritius National Assembly, Hansard, 14 May 2019, answer on the Economic Development Board (Film Rebate Scheme) Regulations 2018](https://mauritiusassembly.govmu.org/mauritiusassembly/wp-admin/admin-ajax.php?action=wpfd&juwpfisadmin=false&preview=1&task=file.download&token=&wpfd_category_id=364&wpfd_file_id=7988); [EDB submission guidance, p. 3](https://edbmauritius.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Guideline-Online-Application-FRS.pdf).
- **Effective date/version:** Regulations 2018; official parliamentary explanation 14 May 2019; EDB guidance dated 31 January 2020 and still reflected on the current EDB page.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Canonically represent 30% as the baseline and 40% as a discretionary ceiling. Any modeled intermediate rate requires project-specific official approval evidence, not a generic tier lookup.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** The 30%-to-40% mechanism is canonical; the selected intermediate percentage is project-specific.

### 1.3 Is Little Utopia's 35% assumption officially supported?

- **Rule/question:** Whether 35% is an official production-independent rate.
- **Authoritative answer:** **No published official authority creates a 35% tier.** A 35% Little Utopia rate can be supported only by a project-specific provisional/final certificate, Film Rebate Committee recommendation, or equivalent EDB approval. Without that evidence it is merely a project assumption.
- **Exact source:** Same National Assembly Hansard and EDB guidance cited in §1.2; both define 30% and a discretionary amount up to 40%, not 35%.
- **Effective date/version:** Same as §1.2.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Do not encode 35% canonically. Label it assumed until a Little Utopia-specific EDB instrument substantiates it.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Project-specific assumption unless an official Little Utopia instrument is supplied.

## 2. Greece

### 2.1 Does the current cash rebate impose an 80%-of-total-production-budget eligible-spend cap, and what is its base?

- **Rule/question:** Whether Greek eligible spend is limited to 80%, and 80% of what.
- **Authoritative answer:** **Yes.** The cap is 80% of the audiovisual work's **total production cost for all production work**, not 80% of Greek spend and not 80% of listed eligible categories alone. The current JMD expressly says total production cost is not confined to the types of expenditure incurred in Greece. The 40% rebate applies to the lesser eligible territorial amount after that ceiling.
- **Exact current official rule:** “implemented within the Greek territory and not exceeding eighty percent (80%) of the total production cost for the entirety of the audiovisual production work.”
- **Exact source:** [JMD 607434, Government Gazette B' 87/14.01.2026, arts. 4–5](https://www.ekkomed.gr/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/KYA-607434-FTV.pdf); [EKKOMED current CRGR-FTV program page](https://www.ekkomed.gr/audiovisual-production-invest/cash-rebate-film-tv-animations-documentaries/); [EKKOMED current legal framework, including JMD 140524](https://www.ekkomed.gr/legal-framework-film-tv/).
- **Effective date/version:** JMD 607434, Gazette B' 87/14 January 2026, as amended by JMD 140524, Gazette B' 2204/20 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Cap eligible Greek production expenditure at `80% × total worldwide production cost`, then apply 40%. Do not use Greek QPE as the cap base.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical rule.

### 2.2 What are the current minimum-spend and total-budget thresholds?

- **Rule/question:** Current CRGR-FTV entry floors.
- **Authoritative answer:** The current paired floors are:

  | Work | Minimum eligible Greek spend | Minimum total production budget |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Fiction film / TV film | €200,000 | €400,000 |
  | Creative documentary film / TV film | €60,000 | €120,000 |
  | Short film, any content | €45,000 | €90,000 |
  | Fiction mini-series, up to 16 episodes | €120,000 per episode | €240,000 per episode |
  | Other fiction series, 17–150 episodes (up to 187 in season two) | €35,000 per episode | €70,000 per episode |
  | Documentary series | €25,000 per episode | €50,000 per episode |

- **Exact source:** [JMD 607434, art. 6 and its threshold table](https://www.ekkomed.gr/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/KYA-607434-FTV.pdf); [Law 5105/2024, Part C](https://www.ekkomed.gr/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/FEK-2024-Tefxos-A-00061-downloaded-30_04_2024_en_fin.pdf); [EKKOMED current minimum-spend guidance](https://www.ekkomed.gr/el/faq/yparchi-elachisto-i-megisto-poso-epileximon-dapanon/).
- **Effective date/version:** Current framework: JMD 607434 as amended through 20 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Enforce both the territorial eligible-spend floor and the corresponding total-budget floor for the applicable format; do not treat one as a substitute for the other.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical thresholds; format and episode count are project facts.

## 3. United Kingdom

### 3.1 Does IFTC exist as a distinct current program or treatment from standard AVEC?

- **Rule/question:** Correct legal identity of IFTC.
- **Authoritative answer:** It exists as a distinct **enhanced AVEC treatment and BFI certification route**, but not as a separate statutory tax credit. “IFTC” is the industry name; the legislation calls the films **certified low-budget films** and grants them AVEC at an enhanced percentage.
- **Exact source:** [HMRC CREC021110](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec021110); [BFI, About UK creative-industry expenditure credits](https://www.bfi.org.uk/apply-british-certification-expenditure-credits/about-uk-creative-industry-expenditure-credits/); [Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, s. 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/12/pdfs/ukpga_20240012_en.pdf).
- **Effective date/version:** Principal photography and qualifying expenditure from 1 April 2024; claims from 1 April 2025; HMRC manual updated 9 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Model IFTC as an enhanced certified-low-budget-film branch within AVEC, not as an unrelated incentive program that could be stacked with standard AVEC.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical program architecture; certification is project-specific.

### 3.2 Current IFTC rate, eligibility, threshold and cultural/co-production requirements

- **Rule/question:** Current enhanced AVEC conditions.
- **Authoritative answer:** The gross credit rate is **53%**. The film must satisfy ordinary film AVEC conditions, obtain a BFI low-budget certificate, have principal photography beginning on/after 1 April 2024, have total core expenditure **£23.5 million or less**, and meet the creative-connection condition through a UK writer, UK director, or official co-production. British qualification is through the cultural-test/certification route or an official co-production, and theatrical-release and minimum 10% UK core-spend conditions apply. Only the first **£15 million of relevant global expenditure** can enter the enhanced-credit calculation.
- **Exact source:** [HMRC CREC021110](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec021110); [HMRC CREC021120](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec021120); [BFI current AVEC/Enhanced AVEC eligibility guidance](https://www.bfi.org.uk/apply-british-certification-expenditure-credits/about-uk-creative-industry-expenditure-credits/).
- **Effective date/version:** As above; current £23.5 million budget condition reflected in HMRC/BFI guidance checked 9 August 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Use 53% as the statutory gross credit rate, enforce the £23.5 million total-core-budget ceiling and £15 million relevant-global-expenditure cap, and gate the treatment on BFI certification plus the creative connection. Do not substitute an “independent producer” ownership test; HMRC says studio ownership is not determinative.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Conditions and rates are canonical; certificate, budget and creative connection are project facts.

### 3.3 Standard AVEC, VFX rates and the 80% cap

- **Rule/question:** Current non-IFTC AVEC computation.
- **Authoritative answer:** Standard live-action film and HETV AVEC is **34%**; animation and children's TV are **39%**. Ordinary qualifying expenditure is the lesser of UK relevant global expenditure and **80% of total relevant global expenditure/core expenditure**. For eligible non-independent, non-animated films and HETV, relevant UK VFX work is relieved at **39%** and is exempt from the 80% cap. Independent films cannot claim the separate VFX uplift because their qualifying costs receive 53% treatment.
- **Exact source:** [HMRC CREC061300](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec061300); [HMRC CREC060100](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec060100); [HMRC CREC061410](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec061410); [HMRC CREC061430](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec061430).
- **Effective date/version:** AVEC from 1 January 2024; enhanced VFX expenditure from 1 January 2025; HMRC manual updated 9 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Store gross statutory credit rates, apply the 80% cap to ordinary AVEC, and separately exempt qualifying UK VFX expenditure only for eligible 34%-rate productions. Do not stack VFX uplift with IFTC.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical; production type and qualifying VFX location are project facts.

## 4. Malta

### 4.1 Are there objective criteria or points for the live-action uplift from 30% to 40%?

- **Rule/question:** Whether the extra 10% is objectively scored or purely discretionary.
- **Authoritative answer:** The current official guideline divides the uplift into two **5% limbs**: (a) Malta features as Malta or local use of facilities; and (b) maximisation of local resources. Annex 1 supplies objective minimum local-crew percentages by department for limb (b)—for example Production 80%, Direction 60%, Locations & Unit 90%, Camera 50%, and Transport 90%, each “if and when applicable.” It does **not** provide a points total, an automatic-award formula, or numeric criteria for the Malta-as-Malta/local-facilities limb. The operative text says the Film Commissioner **has discretion** to award the additional 10%; the local-resource condition is reassessed at final submission and the final percentage is confirmed after MFC assessment and audit.
- **Exact source:** [Screen Malta Film & TV Financial Incentives Guidelines 2024, §3.4 and Annex 1](https://screenmalta.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Screen-Malta-Financial-Incentives-FILM-TV-GUIDELINES-UPDATES.pdf); [Screen Malta current FAQ](https://screenmalta.com/faq/).
- **Effective date/version:** Guidelines published July 2024; scheme stated by Screen Malta to operate through 31 December 2028.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Treat 30% as the live-action base. Treat each potential 5% as conditional and Commissioner-awarded, never automatic. Annex 1 percentages are evidence criteria for the local-resource limb, not a self-executing points test; final certificate percentage controls.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** The two limbs, crew benchmarks and discretion are canonical; whether a production earns either 5% is project-specific.

## 5. Territoriality: authoritative qualifying-expenditure tests

### 5.1 Mauritius

- **Rule/question:** What makes expenditure local/qualifying?
- **Authoritative answer:** The applicant must be incorporated or registered in Mauritius, but the substantive test is QPE **incurred locally** in the EDB's listed production categories. The guidance can include locally performed labour by non-nationals and specified inbound travel; nationality/residency is therefore not a universal test. A Mauritian payer alone does not convert an overseas service into locally incurred QPE.
- **Exact source:** [EDB Film Rebate submission guidance, pp. 3–5](https://edbmauritius.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Guideline-Online-Application-FRS.pdf); [current EDB program page](https://edbmauritius.org/creative-industry).
- **Effective date/version:** Guidance dated 31 January 2020; current program page checked 9 August 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Require local incurrence and category evidence in addition to eligible applicant status; do not use payer/SPV or vendor registration alone.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical test; place of work/incurrence is project evidence.

### 5.2 Malta

- **Rule/question:** What makes expenditure Malta spend?
- **Authoritative answer:** Costs must be eligible costs **incurred in Malta** for the qualifying production and supported by fiscal invoices, contracts, proof of payment, permits and location/personnel records. Documents generally must be in the qualifying company's or approved production-service company's name, but that is an evidentiary/incurrence requirement, not a substitute for Malta activity. Foreign crew may qualify while working in Malta subject to the scheme's employment and withholding requirements; residence is not the universal test.
- **Exact source:** [Screen Malta Guidelines 2024, eligible-expenditure and final-submission provisions, including footnote 126](https://screenmalta.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Screen-Malta-Financial-Incentives-FILM-TV-GUIDELINES-UPDATES.pdf); [Screen Malta FAQ](https://screenmalta.com/faq/).
- **Effective date/version:** July 2024 guidelines; current through the stated 31 December 2028 scheme end unless amended.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Require both an approved claimant/payment chain and evidence that the relevant work or eligible Malta cost occurred in Malta. Do not qualify foreign work merely because it is re-invoiced through a Maltese SPV.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical test; invoice, worksite and permit facts are project-specific.

### 5.3 Greece

- **Rule/question:** What makes expenditure Greek-territorial?
- **Authoritative answer:** Eligible production expenses must be **implemented/incurred in Greek territory** and fall within the eligible categories. Foreign producers generally access the scheme through an agreement with a company established or branched in Greece acting as executive producer. That beneficiary structure is necessary but not sufficient: territorial performance/incurrence remains controlling. The current JMD contains a narrow documentary exception permitting foreign-issued invoices for the director and two leading cast roles on projects with eligible expenses above €8 million, subject to its conditions; this confirms that payer or invoice domicile is not itself the general territorial test.
- **Exact source:** [JMD 607434, arts. 4–7 and Annex II](https://www.ekkomed.gr/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/KYA-607434-FTV.pdf); [EKKOMED current CRGR-FTV beneficiary guidance](https://www.ekkomed.gr/audiovisual-production-invest/cash-rebate-film-tv-animations-documentaries/).
- **Effective date/version:** JMD 607434 as amended through 20 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Test each cost for Greek territorial implementation/incurrence and category eligibility. Do not treat a Greek executive producer or SPV payment as converting foreign performance into Greek spend.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical test; performance, invoice and exception facts are project-specific.

### 5.4 United Kingdom

- **Rule/question:** What makes expenditure UK expenditure for AVEC?
- **Authoritative answer:** UK expenditure is expenditure on goods or services **used or consumed in the UK**. Supplier nationality and location are not determinative; the production company must identify the nature of the item and where the recipient uses or consumes it, with just-and-reasonable apportionment where mixed. Post/VFX is tested independently of photography; the enhanced VFX amount additionally requires the VFX work itself to be carried out in the UK.
- **Exact source:** [HMRC CREC054100](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec054100); [HMRC CREC054220](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec054220); [HMRC CREC054230](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec054230); [HMRC CREC061420](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/creative-industries-expenditure-credit-manual/crec061420).
- **Effective date/version:** AVEC territorial rules from 1 January 2024; HMRC manual updated 9 April 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Use the use/consumption and fair-apportionment tests, not payer, vendor domicile or worker nationality. Apply the narrower UK-work test to the special VFX uplift.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical tests; use/consumption and apportionment are project facts.

### 5.5 Australia

- **Rule/question:** What makes expenditure QAPE for the Location Offset?
- **Authoritative answer:** QAPE is the applicant company's production expenditure reasonably attributable to **goods or services provided in Australia**, use of Australian land, or use of goods located in Australia when used to make the production. The applicant must genuinely incur and be legally liable for the cost. A foreign applicant must have an Australian permanent establishment and active ABN throughout the relevant period. Overseas costs and overseas services embodied in goods are excluded; mixed services are apportioned. Residence is relevant to applicant eligibility and some specific personnel rules, but vendor residence or an Australian SPV payment does not replace the place-of-provision/use test.
- **Exact source:** [Office for the Arts Location Offset Guidelines, April 2025, §§2.1–2.3](https://www.arts.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/location-offset-guidelines-april-2025.pdf); [Location Offset Glossary, April 2025, “QAPE” and “Services embodied in goods”](https://www.arts.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/location-offset-glossary-april-2025.pdf); Income Tax Assessment Act 1997, ss. 376-145 and 376-160, as reproduced and linked in those official manuals.
- **Effective date/version:** April 2025 official guidelines and glossary, current when checked 9 August 2026.
- **Status:** **RESOLVED**.
- **Required CineGlobe rule implication:** Require genuine applicant incurrence plus Australian provision/use/location. Apportion mixed work and exclude overseas embodied services; never infer QAPE from the payment route alone.
- **Project-specific vs canonical rule:** Canonical test; provision, location, duration and applicant-incurrence facts are project-specific.
